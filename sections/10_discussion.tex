\subsection{When additional physiology is justified}

The discussion will connect any gain to a decision that changed and a collaboration outcome that followed, rather than treating more sensing as inherently valuable. The interpretive heterogeneity documented in team physiology and multimodal measurement research makes negative and context-dependent findings plausible ~\cite{p32,p23,p57}.

\subsection{Agency and the agent as part of the team}

Agent participation can itself alter the interaction being sensed: research manipulating the perceived identity of a teammate reports changes in physiology, communication, and performance ~\cite{p53}. We therefore propose logging intervention onset and subsequent responses, separating pre-intervention evidence from consequences. Expectations and human-AI interaction work further motivate consent, refusal, correction, and transparent limits on initiative ~\cite{expect,p3}.

\subsection{Limitations and reproducibility}

Likely boundaries to examine include sensor artifacts, individual and contextual variation, task-specific labels, laboratory ecological validity, and uneven access to eye tracking or private output channels; these concerns are grounded in wearable sensing, proactive interaction, and multimodal collaboration research ~\cite{artifacts,hhiss,crop,speaker,p57}. Which limitations materially affect the findings must be determined from the study. The planned reproducibility package will include a codebook, feature definitions, prompts, model configurations, analysis scripts, and appropriately consented or de-identified derived data.
